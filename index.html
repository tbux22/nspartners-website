MoneyWise

In partnership with

MoneyWise, a financial education and matched-savings program designed to help participants build financial knowledge, establish savings habits, and pursue long-term stability. Conceived, designed, launched and managed by Trajah Buxton at Open Doors.

What the MoneyWise program looked like.

Participants build savings while gaining practical financial knowledge through six workshops over twelve weeks.

Notes
  1. Participants contribute $67 a month for six months, $402 in all (shown rounded).
  2. Open Doors adds $600 at month six, bringing the total to $1,002. Participants may withdraw at that point (Option 1).
  3. Participants who open a qualifying MSCU account and keep the $1,002 in a CD from month 6 to month 12 become eligible for an MSCU match (Option 2). The $1,002 match shown is illustrative; the match is capped at $1,500.
  4. The $2,004 year-one total is an example, not an actual cohort payout.

From real estate finance to community programs

After spending much of his career in real estate finance, Trajah Buxton brought his institutional experience into the nonprofit sector, and in just one year, transformed that experience into innovative programs and partnerships designed to expand economic opportunity.

A complete program. Built around your community.

Match & Grow gives partners the operating infrastructure to connect financial learning with action. NSP designs, coordinates, recruits, measures, and reports, customized to each population.

  1. PartnerStart with people. Define the population, need, geography and funding capacity.
  2. DesignCurriculum map, match structure, intake, assessments and trackers.
  3. RecruitOutreach, partner referrals, messaging and a tracked enrollment funnel.
  4. LaunchWorkshops, savings, coaching, incentives and accountability.
  5. MeasureAttendance, savings, knowledge, confidence and well-being, reported to partners.

Financial-institution professionals, coaches, and community partners deliver instruction. NSP oversees program design, coordination, quality, measurement, and reporting; Trajah participates in selected workshops.

Why partners join.

  1. 01

    Community impact

    Help households build savings, strengthen financial capability, establish banking relationships, and access mainstream financial products.

  2. 02

    CRA alignment

    Programs can be structured around documented financial-education, community-development, and low- or moderate-income service activities that may receive CRA consideration when applicable. Credit is not guaranteed.

  3. 03

    Relationship development

    Connect employees, community organizations, and consumers through workshops, coaching, account relationships, and measurable outcomes.

  1. 01

    A complete program, ready to run

    NSP designs, coordinates, recruits, measures, and reports, customized to the population you serve.

  2. 02

    Free to your participants

    Financial institutions and sponsors fund program design, operations, and participant incentives, so the people you serve never pay.

  3. 03

    Outcomes you can report

    Attendance, savings, knowledge, confidence and well-being are tracked and reported back, ready for funders and boards.

  1. 01

    Resident progress

    Match & Grow can support financial empowerment, asset building, employment and income progress, savings, coaching, and partner coordination for residents.

  2. 02

    HUD FSS: Family Self-Sufficiency

    As earned income rises, an FSS escrow savings account builds. Match & Grow can run alongside an FSS plan with financial education, a matched-savings habit, coaching and banking access.

  3. 03

    HUD ROSS: Resident Opportunities

    ROSS funds service coordinators who connect residents to services. Match & Grow gives them a ready program to refer into, with outcomes reported back.

The model can be structured around FSS and ROSS objectives; it does not automatically fulfill program requirements. Founder experience includes prior work with Norwalk Housing Authority ↗.
